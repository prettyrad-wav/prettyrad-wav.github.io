Your elevator pitch has a strong foundation. You clearly introduce yourself, explain your previous HVAC experience, connect those transferable skills to software development, mention your technical stack, and explain what you are looking for professionally.



For the content, I would recommend making the pitch slightly more specific and personal. Your HVAC background is one of the strongest and most memorable parts of your story, so take advantage of it. Instead of mentioning problem-solving twice, briefly explain how your experience troubleshooting real-world problems translates into how you approach software development. If possible, also mention one project or type of application you have built using JavaScript, React, or the MERN stack. This would make your technical experience more concrete.



The ending could also be a little stronger. Rather than focusing mainly on continuing to learn and grow, communicate what you can already bring to a team while still showing that you are motivated to develop your skills further.



For the delivery, try to make the pitch feel more conversational and less like you are reading a prepared text. Learn the main ideas you want to communicate rather than memorizing every sentence. This will help you sound more natural, confident, and engaging.



For the visual presentation:

Face the camera directly rather than positioning your body diagonally.
Position the camera approximately at eye level. When the camera is too low, it can create an unnatural perspective and make the audience feel as though they are looking up at you.
Smile more, both with your facial expression and your voice. This will make you appear more approachable and enthusiastic.
Use natural body language and hand gestures instead of remaining completely still.
Your lighting and background are very good, and they work well with what you are wearing.
Try not to read too much. Maintain more eye contact with the camera and speak naturally.
If the format allows it, consider adding some dynamic editing or brief visuals showing your projects, applications, code, or technical skills while you are speaking. This could make the video more engaging and provide evidence of what you can do.




Overall, the information in the pitch is good. The next step is making the presentation feel less rehearsed and more like a confident conversation with a potential employer. Your previous professional experience gives you an interesting story; make that personality and experience more visible in both your words and your delivery.
